Cultures that share far more mt lines than Y lines #

Pairs of cultures with a shared sample country (3+ samples from it in each), the younger at most 3,000 years after the older. We take cultures with at least 30 Y and 30 mt samples and count shared lines at the depth of the tree the comparison page picks for each pair by itself. Shown are pairs where mt shares at least 30 points more lines than Y, and Y shares no more than 25%. This looks like new men arriving among local women, but different samples look the same way: the male and female samples may come from different burial grounds, or from relatives buried together. Across all 296 pairs the median difference is 17 points. Each older culture keeps only its pair with the largest difference. We check each gap against chance: up to 10,000 times we reshuffle the samples between the two cultures, keeping group sizes, and keep the pairs where such a gap rarely comes up. The correction covers all 296 pairs, with a false discovery rate of 5%.
