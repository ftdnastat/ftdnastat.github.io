About the data

Sources: public FamilyTreeDNA tree dumps (Y-DNA Haplotree and mtDNA Haplotree) and the Discover API for branch ages. Dumps are downloaded weekly; the site is rebuilt from the fresh dumps and published through an acceptance gate.

A kit's country is the origin of the earliest known ancestor on that line, entered by the FTDNA customer. It is not a place of residence and not a population sample: the US, Britain and Scandinavia are over-represented, Africa and Asia are nearly empty, and some countries are skewed by diaspora. Spain is roughly 18% Native American by mtDNA because Latin Americans report their colonial ancestors' country; Belarus and Lithuania are 15–20% Ashkenazi.

Kits without a country and those marked Unknown Origin are excluded: 61% of Y kits and 74% of mt kits. The unknowns contain more African lineages than kits with a country, so world L shares are understated.

One entity is deliberately relabelled: “Italy (Aosta Valley)” is shown as Orstkhoy, a people closely related to the Chechens and Ingush who report this region because of the similar flags. Details on its page.

Subregions are separate entities: “Russia (Republic of Dagestan)” is not part of “Russian Federation”, “Spain (Catalonia)” is not part of “Spain”. The parent is shown as a link.

A bare node (J-M172, H, I2) means the kit is not typed deeper: an STR test or an old SNP pack on Y, an HVR test without full sequencing on mt. In the major-clade view the asterisk collects kits under a node that fall into none of its major subclades.

Nested clades are never shown side by side as siblings: either a cumulative tree where a node counts everything below it, or mutually exclusive buckets with an asterisk.

Branch age is the TMRCA estimated by FTDNA Discover, the midpoint rounded to a century; above 10,000 years it is rounded up to a whole thousand. mt branch ages come from FTDNA's Mitotree where the branch exists there.

One reliability threshold for the whole site: samples under 50 kits are flagged as small and excluded from findings; closest countries need 100+ kits, a signature branch needs at least 10 kits.

YFull data #

The second source is the public YFull trees (Y tree and mt tree), snapshot of 2026-09-29, refreshed monthly. YFull is a paid analysis of full Y chromosomes and mt genomes, so the sample is skewed towards enthusiasts and projects even more than FTDNA's: Saudi Arabia contributes more samples than any other country.

Comparisons with FTDNA pages use only YFull customers with a YF number and only modern samples: ancient DNA and samples from GenBank and publications are excluded, because FTDNA counts customers only. For mt fewer than a third of YFull samples qualify, so mt comparisons are thinner than Y.

The YFull and FTDNA trees branch differently and name nodes after different SNPs. A YFull sample is placed on the FTDNA tree through SNPs the two trees share: 88% of YFull nodes find an FTDNA node directly, the rest through the nearest resolved ancestor. Level shares are then computed with the same code as for FTDNA kits.

Country and region on YFull, as on FTDNA, are reported by the tested person. Region codes are mapped to FTDNA entities by hand: republics of Russia, Spanish autonomies through provinces, Italian regions, parts of the United Kingdom. Samples from a region that is an FTDNA entity of its own are taken out of the country sample.

Language on YFull is the tested person's self-identification by ISO 639-3; FTDNA has no such field. Language is neither ethnicity nor region: Chechens of Jordan are counted with Chechens of Chechnya, and “Arabic” spans every Arab country.

The ypredictor tool #

Sister project ypredictor.github.io: paste your FTDNA STR markers to get a predicted Y-tree branch with the closest matches. Match countries there link to pages of this site, and from here you can test your haplogroup from markers.
