Kushan Empire

The Kushan Empire (c. 30–c. 375 CE) was a syncretic empire formed by the Yuezhi in the early 1st century CE. Initially based in Bactria, it spread over much of what is now Afghanistan, India, Pakistan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. The Kushans were most probably one of five branches of the Yuezhi confederation, a nomadic Indo-European people of possible Tocharian origin who came from northwestern China. They were great patrons of Buddhism and helped spread it to Central Asia and China. The empire had diplomatic contacts with Rome, Sasanian Persia, the Aksumite Empire and Han China, and in the 3rd century it fragmented into semi-independent kingdoms.
