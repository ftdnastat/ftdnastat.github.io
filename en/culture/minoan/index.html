Minoan civilization

The Minoan civilization was one of the main Aegean civilizations of the Bronze Age; it existed on Crete from 2700 to 1400 BCE. Its focus was the palaces, which served as complex economic and political centers; the largest stood at Knossos, Phaistos, Zakros and Tylissos. The Minoans traded widely by sea and used two scripts, Cretan hieroglyphs and Linear A, neither of which has been fully deciphered. The civilization is named after the mythical king Minos; it was discovered in 1900 by the British archaeologist Arthur Evans, who began excavating the palace of Knossos. Ancient DNA has shown that Minoans and Mycenaeans were genetically close: at least three quarters of their ancestry goes back to the first Neolithic farmers of western Anatolia and the Aegean.

Sources: Минойская цивилизация — Википедия; Minoan civilization — Wikipedia; Lazaridis I. et al. (2017). Genetic origins of the Minoans and Mycenaeans. Nature
