Volosovo culture

The Volosovo culture is a Neolithic archaeological culture of the 4th–3rd millennia BCE, spread across central Russia and the Volga region. V. A. Gorodtsov identified it from the Volosovo and Panfilovo sites he excavated; the name comes from a site found by O. N. Bader near the village of Volosovo in the Nizhny Novgorod region, close to the mouth of the Veletma, a tributary of the Oka. The culture followed the Neolithic Pit-marked pottery culture (Balakhna), and its finds are related to those of the middle Volga and the Kama basin. It emerged between the 4th and 3rd millennia BCE and lasted into the 2nd millennium; its people have been described as forest foragers. Anthropologically the population was Europoid with a Lapponoid admixture.

Sources: Волосовская культура — Википедия; Volosovo culture — Wikipedia
