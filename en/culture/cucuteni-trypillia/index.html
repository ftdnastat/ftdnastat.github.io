Cucuteni–Trypillia culture

The Trypillia culture belongs to the Eneolithic and the Early Bronze Age; its lands are Moldova and the Ukrainian forest-steppe and steppe from the Dnieper valley to the southeastern Carpathian foothills. In Romania its counterpart is the Cucuteni culture. It existed from about 5050 to 2950 BCE. The Trypillians were farmers and herders and knew copper and gold. Their largest settlements spread over hundreds of hectares and had up to 2,000 dwellings. Trypillia was succeeded by the Yamnaya community of nomadic herders. The genomes of four women from two late sites in Moldova (3500–3100 BCE) showed a considerable share of Neolithic ancestry and, in three of them, steppe ancestry as well.

Sources: Трипольская культура — Википедия; Cucuteni–Trypillia culture — Wikipedia; Immel A. et al. (2020). Gene-flow from steppe individuals into Cucuteni-Trypillia associated populations indicates long-standing contacts and gradual admixture. Scientific Reports
