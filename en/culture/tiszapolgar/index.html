Tiszapolgár culture

The Tiszapolgár culture (4500–4000 BCE) was an Eneolithic archaeological culture of Central Europe in the Carpathian Basin, on the Great Hungarian Plain. It was located in present-day eastern Hungary, eastern Slovakia, Transcarpathian Ukraine and western Romania. Most of the information comes from cemeteries; over 150 graves have been excavated at the type site, Tiszapolgár-Basatanya. A study of the genomes of 125 people from the Carpathian Basin (4800–3900 BCE) showed a high degree of genetic continuity from the Late Neolithic to the Early Copper Age despite changes in settlement, burial practice and material culture.

Sources: Tiszapolgár culture — Wikipedia; Szécsényi-Nagy A. et al. (2025). Ancient DNA reveals diverse community organizations in the 5th millennium BCE Carpathian Basin. Nature Communications
