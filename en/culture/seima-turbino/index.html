Seima-Turbino culture

The Seima-Turbino phenomenon is a pattern of burial sites with similar bronze artefacts, found across northern Eurasia, especially Siberia and Central Asia, and possibly from Fennoscandia to Mongolia. Recent studies date it to about 2200–1900 BCE, while Russian scholarship gives 2150–1600 BCE. The thin-walled tin bronze came from ore mined in the south-eastern Altai and Xinjiang, and the Altai Mountains are regarded as the homeland. The buried were nomadic warriors and metalworkers who travelled on horseback or in two-wheeled carts. The name comes from the Seyma cemetery near the confluence of the Oka and Volga, excavated from about 1914, and the Turbino cemetery in Perm, excavated from 1924.

Sources: Сейминско-турбинский феномен — Википедия; Seima-Turbino culture — Wikipedia
