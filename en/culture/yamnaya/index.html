Yamnaya culture

The Yamnaya culture was an archaeological culture of the Late Copper Age to the Early Bronze Age, dated roughly to 3300–2600 BCE. It occupied the steppe from the southern Urals to the Dniester and from the Ciscaucasus to the middle Volga. Its economy was based on nomadic herding, and the earliest remains of four-wheeled wagons in Eastern Europe come from Yamnaya kurgan burials. The dead were buried in pits under kurgans, lying on the back with bent knees and covered in ochre. Paleogenetics shows that the Yamnaya descended from Eastern European hunter-gatherers and a population with Near Eastern ancestry, and that Corded Ware people from Germany traced about 75% of their ancestry to them.

Sources: Ямная культура — Википедия; Yamnaya culture — Wikipedia; Haak W. et al. (2015). Massive migration from the steppe was a source for Indo-European languages in Europe. Nature
