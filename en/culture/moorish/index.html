Moors

Moors is an exonym used in European languages to designate primarily the Muslim populations of North Africa and the Iberian Peninsula, particularly al-Andalus, during the Middle Ages. The Moors were not a single, distinct or self-defined people: medieval and early modern Europeans applied the name variously to Arabs, Berbers, Muslim Europeans and black peoples. The 1911 Encyclopædia Britannica observed that the term had no real ethnological value, and since the mid-20th century it has fallen out of fashion among scholars because of its racial connotations. The word is also used for other groups, such as the Ceylon Moors and Indian Moors of South Asia and, in Mauritania, the black and white Moors.
