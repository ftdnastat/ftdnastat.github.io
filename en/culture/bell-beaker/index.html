Bell Beaker culture

The Bell Beaker culture was an archaeological culture of the Copper Age to the Early Bronze Age, named after the inverted-bell drinking vessel. It is dated roughly to 2800–1900 BCE. It spread from the Iberian Peninsula to the British Isles and the Danubian plains. Early maritime-style beakers are known from the Tagus estuary. Typical artefacts are copper daggers, stone archer's wrist-guards and arrowheads; craftsmen worked copper, arsenical bronze and gold. Paleogenetic data show that in Britain the spread of the Beaker complex brought steppe-related ancestry and the replacement of about 90% of the gene pool.

Sources: Культура колоколовидных кубков — Википедия; Bell Beaker culture — Wikipedia; Olalde I. et al. (2018). The Beaker phenomenon and the genomic transformation of northwest Europe. Nature
