Paleo-Indians

Paleo-Indians were the first peoples to enter and inhabit the Americas, towards the end of the Late Pleistocene. According to traditional theories, big-animal hunters crossed from North Asia over the Beringia land bridge, which existed from 45,000 to 12,000 BCE, and small groups of hunter-gatherers followed herds of large herbivores far into Alaska. From about 16,500 to 13,500 BCE, ice-free corridors opened along the Pacific coast and in North American valleys, and people moved south on foot or by boat along the coastline. Stone tools, especially projectile points and scrapers, are the main evidence of their presence. They lived alongside many megafauna species that mostly died out by the end of the Paleoindian period.

Sources: Палеоиндейцы — Википедия; Paleo-Indians — Wikipedia
