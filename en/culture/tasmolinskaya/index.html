Tasmola culture

The Tasmola culture was an Early Iron Age culture of central Kazakhstan from the Saka period, and it was replaced by the Korgantas culture. It is named after the Tasmola area, and Russian scholarship dates the Early Iron Age there to the 7th–2nd centuries BCE. Its development is divided into early (7th–6th centuries BCE), middle (5th–3rd) and late (3rd–1st) stages. The hallmark of its sites is the so-called mustached kurgans: a main mound with a burial, a second mound that usually holds a horse, and two arc-shaped stone ridges always stretching east. Archaeologists see a solar cult in this layout. The Tasmola people may correspond to the Issedones of Greek sources.
