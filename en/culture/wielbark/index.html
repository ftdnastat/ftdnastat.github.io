Wielbark culture

The Wielbark culture was an Iron Age archaeological complex on the territory of present-day Poland, which emerged in the 1st century CE on the lower Vistula, around Gdańsk and Chełmno, lasted until the 5th century and later expanded southward. It displays cultural links with southern Scandinavia. It is distinguished from the preceding Oksywie culture by the adoption of inhumation, although cremation also occurs. It is also called the Gotho-Gepidic culture; Roman authors placed the Gutones, Rugii and Lemovii in the same area. Ancient DNA suggests that its population was formed by immigrants from the north who entered Poland at the beginning of the first millennium CE and mixed with the local inhabitants.

Sources: Вельбарская культура — Википедия; Wielbark culture — Wikipedia; Stolarek I. et al. (2023). Genetic history of East-Central Europe in the first millennium CE. Genome Biology
