Iberomaurusian

The Iberomaurusian, also called the Oranian or Ouchtata culture, is an Epipaleolithic backed-bladelet industry of North Africa, found near the coasts of Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia. In Libya it is known from a single major site, Haua Fteah, where it is called the Eastern Oranian. It seems to have appeared around the Last Glacial Maximum, between about 25,000 and 23,000 cal BP, and lasted until the early Holocene, about 11,000 cal BP. The name means "of Iberia and Mauretania"; Pallary coined it in 1909, believing the industry reached the Iberian Peninsula, an idea now discounted. In Algeria, Tunisia and Libya it was succeeded by the Capsian, and in Morocco and western Algeria by the Cardial culture after a long hiatus.

Sources: Иберо-мавританская культура — Википедия; Iberomaurusian — Wikipedia
