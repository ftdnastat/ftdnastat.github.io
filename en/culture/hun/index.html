Huns

The Huns were a people that took shape in the 2nd–5th centuries in the steppes of Eastern Europe; its core is thought to have been Xiongnu who had come from Central Asia, joined by local Ugrians and Sarmatians. By 430 their vast but short-lived realm had risen on the Danubian frontier of the Roman Empire. The Hunnic union centered on Pannonia reached its greatest extent under Attila (434–453) and fell apart after his death in 453. The Huns were nomadic herders; bronze cauldrons and the custom of artificial cranial deformation are associated with them. Paleogeneticists found lineages directly connecting some members of the highest Xiongnu-period elite with people of the Carpathian Basin of the 5th–6th centuries, which means that some European Huns were their descendants.

Sources: Гунны — Википедия; Huns — Wikipedia; Gnecchi-Ruscone G. A. et al. (2025). Ancient genomes reveal trans-Eurasian connections between the European Huns and the Xiongnu Empire. PNAS
