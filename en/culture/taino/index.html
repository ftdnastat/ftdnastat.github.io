Taíno

The Taíno were the collective designation of Arawakan peoples who inhabited the Greater Antilles and the Bahamas before the arrival of Europeans. Columbus encountered them on 12 October 1492. Scholars propose that their ancestors migrated from South America about 2,500 years ago; genetically they are related to speakers of Arawakan languages from northeast South America. They farmed cassava, maize, sweet potato and tobacco in mounds called conucos. Society was divided into caciques, nitaínos, bohiques and naborias. Within about 30 years of the Spanish arrival, an estimated 80–90% of the population died from disease, forced labor and conflict.

Sources: Таино (группа народов) — Википедия; Taíno — Wikipedia; Fernandes D. M. et al. (2021). A genetic history of the pre-contact Caribbean. Nature
