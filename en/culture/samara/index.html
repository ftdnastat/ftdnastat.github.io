Samara culture

The Samara culture is an Eneolithic, or Copper Age, culture dating to the turn of the 5th millennium BCE at the Samara Bend of the Volga River. It was found in 1973 during excavations near the village of Syezzhee in the Bogatovsky district of Samara Oblast, and several more settlements were discovered later. It formed on the basis of the Middle Volga culture of the Middle Neolithic and bordered the Agidel culture to the east. Later cultures in the same area are sometimes also called Samara, while the Samara culture proper is treated as the early Eneolithic culture of the region. It is regarded as related to contemporaneous or later cultures of the Pontic–Caspian steppe, such as Khvalynsk, Repin and Yamnaya.
