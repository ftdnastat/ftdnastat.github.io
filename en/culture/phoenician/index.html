Phoenicians

The Phoenicians were an ancient Semitic maritime civilization of city-states on the Levantine coast, mainly in present-day Lebanon. Their period of greatest prominence was 1200–332 BCE. They created one of antiquity's most extensive maritime trade networks and founded colonies in Sicily, Sardinia, Cyprus, Malta, Iberia and North Africa; Carthage was founded in 814 BCE. Around 1050 BCE the Phoenician alphabet of 22 consonants emerged, and it gave rise to the Greek alphabet. A DNA study of Punic settlements showed that their inhabitants derived little ancestry from Levantine Phoenicians.

Sources: Финикийцы — Википедия; Phoenicia — Wikipedia; Ringbauer H. et al. (2025). Punic people were genetically diverse with almost no Levantine ancestors. Nature
