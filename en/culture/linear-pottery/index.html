Linear Pottery culture

The Linear Pottery culture was the most widespread Neolithic culture of Central Europe, flourishing roughly 5500–4500 BCE. It stretched from the Seine basin in the west to the Dnieper in the east, with the Danube as its southern limit. Its pottery tradition is linked to the Starčevo-Körös culture of the Middle Danube. The economy relied on slash-and-burn farming and stock-raising, with wheat and legumes as crops. People lived in long houses 5.5–7 m wide. Burials varied, from cremations to crouched skeletons sprinkled with ochre. Ancient DNA shows that Neolithic Europeans descended from Anatolian migrants with limited admixture from resident hunter-gatherers.
