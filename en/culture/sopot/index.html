Sopot culture

The Sopot culture is a Neolithic archaeological culture first identified in eastern Slavonia in modern-day Croatia and since found at several sites in Hungary. It continued the Starčevo culture and was strongly influenced by the Vinča culture, dating to around 5000 BCE. From its original area it spread into northern Bosnia, northwestern Croatia and Hungarian Transdanubia, where it helped start the Lengyel culture. Settlements stood on river banks, most notably along the Bosut near Vinkovci, with square wooden houses, sometimes divided into several rooms. Finds include weapons of bone, flint and obsidian and pottery of various sizes. The type site, Sopot near Vinkovci, dates to 5480–3790 BCE; the culture was identified in 1949 by Vladimir Milojčić and named in 1968 by Stojan Dimitrijević.
