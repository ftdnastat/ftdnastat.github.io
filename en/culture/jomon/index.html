Jōmon period

The Jōmon period is the time between about 14,000 and 300 BCE in Japanese history, when Japan was inhabited by the Jōmon people, a diverse hunter-gatherer and early agriculturalist population united by a common culture. They reached a considerable degree of sedentism and cultural complexity, and their civilisation is divided into six phases. Their ancestors migrated from Northeast Asia, the Korean Peninsula, China and Southeast Asia. The period was rich in tools and jewellery of bone, stone, shell and antler, pottery figurines and vessels, and lacquerware. Jōmon pottery is noted for cords pressed into the wet clay, and the Jōmon eventually admixed with the Japonic-speaking Yayoi people.

Sources: Период Дзёмон — Википедия; Jōmon period — Wikipedia
