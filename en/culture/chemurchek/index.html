Chemurchek culture

The Chemurchek culture (2750–1900 BCE) is a Bronze Age archaeological culture of western Mongolia and the borders of neighbouring countries, such as the Dzungarian Basin of Xinjiang and eastern Kazakhstan. It immediately follows the Afanasievo culture and is contemporary with the early Tarim Mummies to the south and the Okunev culture to the north. Its burials are characterized by large rectangular stone fences built around collective tombs. The supine position of the deceased with flexed legs is similar to that of the Afanasievo culture, but the Chemurchek culture is considered distinct. It is named after the Chemurchek cemetery in Altay City, Xinjiang, and its sites reach west to the Ili valley in Kazakhstan.

Sources: Чемурчекская культура — Википедия; Chemurchek culture — Wikipedia
