Saka

Saka is a collective name in ancient sources for a group of Iranian-speaking nomadic and semi-nomadic tribes of the 1st millennium BCE to the first centuries CE. Ancient Greek authors called them "Asiatic Scythians". They are considered descendants of bearers of the Srubnaya and the Alakul variant of the Andronovo cultures. In the 2nd century BCE many Saka were driven by the Yuezhi into Sogdia and Bactria and then into northwestern South Asia, where they became known as Indo-Scythians. Genomes of 111 individuals from the Central Asian Steppe showed that two main Iron Age gene pools, around the Altai and the Urals, rested on major Late Bronze Age admixture.

Sources: Саки (племена) — Википедия; Saka — Wikipedia; Gnecchi-Ruscone G. A. et al. (2021). Ancient genomic time transect from the Central Asian Steppe unravels the history of the Scythians. Science Advances
