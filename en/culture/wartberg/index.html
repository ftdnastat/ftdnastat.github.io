Wartberg culture

The Wartberg culture was a culture of the later Central European Neolithic, lasting roughly 3600–2800 BCE. It is distributed across northern Hesse, southern Lower Saxony and western Thuringia. It is known for megalithic gallery graves containing the remains of many individuals of all ages and sexes; the Altendorf tomb held at least 250 people. The society was sedentary, subsisting on agriculture and animal husbandry, with hunting likely playing a significant role. In the collective burial at Niedertiefenbach (3300–3200 BCE), genomes of 42 individuals showed a surprisingly large hunter-gatherer ancestry component (34–58%).

Sources: Вартберг (культура) — Википедия; Wartberg culture — Wikipedia; Immel A. et al. (2021). Genome-wide study of a Neolithic Wartberg grave community reveals distinct HLA variation and hunter-gatherer ancestry. Communications Biology
