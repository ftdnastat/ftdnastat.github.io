Arras culture

The Arras culture is an archaeological culture of the Middle Iron Age in East Yorkshire, England, named after a cemetery near Market Weighton. Its burials are dated from the latter part of the 1st millennium BCE to the Roman conquest, about 70 CE. Its extent is loosely associated with the Parisi tribe. Characteristic are square barrows containing single inhumations, with bodies laid on a north–south axis, heads to the north. Some burials contain chariots, which were typically disassembled. Grave goods included metalwork, ceramics, coral, jet and enamel. Analysis of the DNA of 534 individuals revealed signs of matrilineal kinship organization.

Sources: Arras culture — Wikipedia; Olalde I. et al. (2026). Ancient DNA reveals matrilineal organisation and recurrent unions between dominant matrilines in Iron Age Britain. bioRxiv (preprint)
