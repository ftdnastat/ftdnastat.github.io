Guanches

Guanches is a generalized name for the indigenous people of the Canary Islands. Strictly, the name belongs only to the natives of Tenerife; the inhabitants of the other islands were called Majoreros, Bimbaches, Auaritas and others. They spoke Guanche (Canarian) languages, most likely close to the Berber languages. They are believed to have arrived on the archipelago in the first millennium BCE. During the Spanish conquest (1402–1496) the Guanches resisted, but most were exterminated or sold into slavery. Genomes of five Guanches of the 7th–11th centuries CE from Gran Canaria and Tenerife showed they were genetically similar over time and closest to present-day Northwest Africans, strongly supporting a Berber-like origin.

Sources: Гуанчи — Википедия; Guanches — Wikipedia; Rodríguez-Varela R. et al. (2017). Genomic analyses of pre-European conquest human remains from the Canary Islands reveal close affinity to modern North Africans. Current Biology
