Visigoths

The Visigoths were an ancient Germanic tribe, the western branch of the Gothic tribal union, which by the mid-3rd century had split into Visigoths and Ostrogoths. Their ancestors were probably the Thervingi: in 376 these crossed into the Roman Empire, in 378 they defeated the Romans at Adrianople, and in 410, led by Alaric I, they sacked Rome. In 418 the Visigoths received land in southern Gaul as foederati, and their kingdom arose there with its capital at Toulouse. After losing to the Franks at Vouillé (507) the kingdom held on in Spain and Portugal, where it stood for two more centuries. A paleogenetic study of Portugal showed that Central European genetic diversity arrived there in the Early Middle Ages, probably with the migrations of Germanic tribes.

Sources: Вестготы — Википедия; Visigoths — Wikipedia; Roca-Rada X. et al. (2025). The genetic history of Portugal over the past 5,000 years. Genome Biology
