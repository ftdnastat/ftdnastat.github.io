Scythians, late period

The Scythians were an ancient Eastern Iranic equestrian nomadic people who migrated during the 9th to 8th centuries BCE from Central Asia to the Pontic Steppe and remained active there until roughly the 3rd century BCE. They occupied the steppes between the lower Danube and the Don, including Crimea. Life was based on nomadic herding; the Scythians exported grain, slaves, honey and furs and imported wine and olive oil. Elites were buried in kurgans over 20 meters high with weapons, luxury goods, horses and killed servants. Weapons and horse harnesses were decorated in Animal Style art. Scythian genomes are described as a mixture of Yamnaya-related and East Asian ancestry.

Sources: Скифы — Википедия; Scythians — Wikipedia; Unterländer M. et al. (2017). Ancestry and demography and descendants of Iron Age nomads of the Eurasian Steppe. Nature Communications
