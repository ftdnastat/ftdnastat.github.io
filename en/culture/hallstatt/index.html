Hallstatt culture

The Hallstatt culture was an Iron Age archaeological culture of Central Europe and the Balkans, dated roughly to 900–400 BCE. Its western part is often referred to as early Celtic. It is named after the Austrian village of Hallstatt, where salt was mined; the salt mines preserved textiles, wood, leather and tools. Elites were buried under tumuli, sometimes on wagons, and the major settlements with elite residences and fortifications are known as princely seats. Genomes of 31 individuals from southern Germany revealed biological relationships between elite burials up to 100 km apart, which may point to matrilineal succession of rulers.

Sources: Гальштатская культура — Википедия; Hallstatt culture — Wikipedia; Gretzinger J. et al. (2024). Evidence for dynastic succession among early Celtic elites in Central Europe. Nature Human Behaviour
