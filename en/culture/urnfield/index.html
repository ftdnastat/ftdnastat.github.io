Urnfield culture

The Urnfield culture (c. 1300–750 BCE) is a collective name for a series of late Bronze Age and early Iron Age cultures of Central Europe and well beyond. The name comes from cemeteries without mounds, mostly of cremation burials, in which the ashes were placed in clay vessels at the bottom of the grave. The culture grew out of the Tumulus culture and was succeeded by the Hallstatt culture; some scholars link it with speakers of a Proto-Celtic language. Around 1000 BCE the south German and Rhenish groups arose in the Danube and Rhine valleys and later spread to France, the Iberian Peninsula and Britain, while in Italy the Golasecca culture was a variant.
