Unterwölbling culture

The Unterwölbling culture is an Early Bronze Age culture that thrived between 2300 and 1800 BCE in the region roughly bounded by the Danube, the Lower Austrian Alpine foothills, the Enns River and the Vienna Woods. Its main localities lie in the lower reaches of Danube tributaries, including the Enns, Ybbs, Melk, Fladnitz, Traisen and Great Tulln. It probably originated from the Late Neolithic Bell Beaker culture and was later replaced by the Böheimkirchen (Veterov) culture. The name was coined in 1937 by Richard Pittioni after the town of Unterwölbling in Lower Austria. Its metalwork was made mainly from forged sheet metal decorated with dots; women's graves held leather caps with strips of bronze sheet and chains of shells, amber, bronze and bone with trapezoidal bone pendants.
