Serovo

The Serovo culture is an archaeological culture of the Siberian Neolithic (4th–3rd millennia BCE), spread across the Angara region, the Upper Lena and, at its late stage, the western shore of Lake Baikal. It was identified by Okladnikov in the 1930s and was succeeded by the Glazkovo culture. The economy rested on elk hunting and fishing; people made wide use of birch bark, probably lived in above-ground dwellings of the chum type and used an improved bow and arrows. Camps have yielded pottery, stone knives, bone needles, boar-tusk ornaments and jade tools. Numerous rock paintings on the stone islands of the Angara depict mainly elk and fish. Genetic studies link the bearers with mitochondrial haplogroups D, G2, C and Z.

Sources: Серовская культура — Википедия
