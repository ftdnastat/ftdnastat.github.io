Yangshao culture

The Yangshao culture was a Neolithic culture that existed extensively along the middle reaches of the Yellow River in China from around 5000 BCE to 3000 BCE. It saw social and technological development in the region, with advances in agriculture, architecture and crafts. It is named after the Yangshao site, discovered in 1921 in western Henan by the Swedish geologist Johan Gunnar Andersson, and it flourished mainly in Henan and the neighbouring provinces of Shaanxi and Shanxi. Its most distinctive trait is painted pottery. Recent research indicates a common origin and spread of the Sino-Tibetan languages with the Cishan, Yangshao and/or Majiayao cultures.

Sources: Яншао — Википедия; Yangshao culture — Wikipedia
