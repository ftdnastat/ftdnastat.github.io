Alans

The Alans were an ancient and medieval Iranic nomadic pastoral people who migrated to the North Caucasus; some continued on to Europe and later North Africa. They are generally regarded as part of the Sarmatians and are mentioned by Roman sources in the 1st century CE, when they had settled north of the Black Sea. After the Huns defeated the Goths around 375 CE, many Alans migrated west along with Germanic tribes, crossing the Rhine in 406 and the Pyrenees around 409. In 428 they crossed with the Vandals into North Africa, where a kingdom lasted until Justinian I conquered it in 534. Those who stayed established the kingdom of Alania in the 9th century, which survived until the Mongol invasions; various scholars see them as ancestors of the Ossetians.
