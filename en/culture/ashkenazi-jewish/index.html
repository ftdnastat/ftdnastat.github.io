Ashkenazi Jews

Ashkenazi Jews are a distinct ethnic subdivision of the Jewish diaspora. They emerged from Jewish communities that consolidated in the 10th century in the Rhineland and northern France, having migrated there from the Italian Peninsula and the Southern Levant. After the massacres of the Crusades they moved gradually east, and the move grew after the persecutions of the Black Death in the 14th century. By the 16th century most Ashkenazim lived in the Kingdom of Poland, which was the main centre of Ashkenazi Jewry until the Holocaust. They follow the German rite and until the Holocaust mostly spoke Yiddish, an offshoot of Middle High German.

Sources: Ашкеназы — Википедия; Ashkenazi Jews — Wikipedia
