Iberian Peninsula: Neolithic Iberian → Chalcolithic Iberian → Bell Beaker culture → Bronze Age Iberian

From the Neolithic to the Copper Age the male lines of Iberia barely change: 57% in common, with I2a, I2b and G2a in the lead both times. The Bell Beaker men share only 14% with the local Copper Age. The peninsula's Bronze Age, by contrast, repeats the Beakers at 80%, and 94% of its samples are R1b.

      How to read the chart

      Each sample is cut to its ancestor at the chosen depth; a sample whose test did not reach that deep stays on its own branch. An asterisk: in this culture the branch also has samples further down. “A → B” is the share of A's samples whose branch occurs in B at least once; the frequency overlap sums the smaller share of every shared branch and is less swayed by single samples. By age, a sample moves up to the ancestor whose branch had already split off by the chosen year (branch ages are Discover estimates), so every letter is cut at the same time. “Auto” takes the time of the oldest culture chosen, or earlier if a column would not read otherwise. Maternal lines follow the Mitotree tree of FTDNA Discover.
