Etruscan civilization

The Etruscans were an ancient civilization of the 1st millennium BCE in the northwest of the Italian Peninsula, between the Arno and the Tiber. Their developed culture was older than the Roman one and influenced it strongly: the Romans took the arch and the vault from the Etruscans, and gladiatorial combat, chariot racing and many funeral rites go back to them as well. The Etruscans spoke a language isolate and wrote it in an alphabet of their own. Their territory was at its largest around 500 BCE, and from the late 4th century BCE it was absorbed by expanding Rome. Ancient DNA shows that Iron Age Etruscans, whose language was presumably not Indo-European, carried a steppe component associated with Indo-Europeans; no recent Anatolian admixture was found in them.

Sources: Этруски — Википедия; Etruscan civilization — Wikipedia; Posth C. et al. (2021). The origin and legacy of the Etruscans through a 2000-year archeogenomic time transect. Science Advances
