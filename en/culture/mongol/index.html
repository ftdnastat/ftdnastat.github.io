Mongol Empire

The Mongol Empire took shape in the 13th century through the conquests of Genghis Khan and his successors. It began with the unification of nomadic tribes under Temüjin: in 1206 a council proclaimed him ruler of all Mongols and gave him the title Genghis Khan. No conquered realm in world history held so large a contiguous territory, from Eastern Europe to the Sea of Japan. The largest states into which it split were the Yuan, the Ulus of Jochi (the Golden Horde), the Ilkhanate and the Chagatai Khanate; the Yuan empire ceased to exist in 1368. Genomes from the Eastern Steppe showed that the Mongols had a much higher share of East Eurasian ancestry than the earlier population of the region and that they are close to present-day Mongolic-speaking peoples.

Sources: Монгольская империя — Википедия; Mongol Empire — Wikipedia; Jeong C. et al. (2020). A dynamic 6,000-year genetic history of Eurasia's Eastern Steppe. Cell
