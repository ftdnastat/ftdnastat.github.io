Lchashen–Metsamor culture

The Lchashen–Metsamor culture is an archaeological culture of the Late Bronze and Early Iron Ages (1500–700 BCE) in the South Caucasus. It was spread mainly in present-day Armenia; its pottery has also been found in southern Georgia. The culture is linked to the Etiuni tribal union known from Urartian and Assyrian sources. Hundreds of fortress settlements and large necropolises indicate a high population density. The decline began in the 8th century BCE, when Argishti I of Urartu crossed the Araxes. A study of Bronze Age Georgia and Armenia found a persisting local gene pool that absorbed additional Anatolian and steppe ancestry in the Middle–Late Bronze Age.

Sources: Лчашен-мецаморская культура — Википедия; Lchashen–Metsamor culture — Wikipedia; Skourtanioti E. et al. (2025). The genetic history of the Southern Caucasus from the Bronze Age to the Early Middle Ages: 5,000 years of genetic continuity despite high mobility. Cell
