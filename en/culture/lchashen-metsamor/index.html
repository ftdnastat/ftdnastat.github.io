Lchashen–Metsamor culture

The Lchashen–Metsamor culture existed in the South Caucasus in the Late Bronze and Early Iron Age (1500–700 BCE). Its sites are concentrated in present-day Armenia, and its pottery is also found in southern Georgia. Hundreds of fortress settlements and large necropolises point to a dense population. The culture is associated with the Etiuni tribal union mentioned in Urartian and Assyrian sources. Its decline began in the 8th century BCE, when the Urartian king Argishti I crossed the Araxes. A paleogenetic study of Bronze Age Georgia and Armenia showed a stable local gene pool to which ancestry from Anatolia and the Eurasian steppes was added in the Middle and Late Bronze Age.

Sources: Лчашен-мецаморская культура — Википедия; Lchashen–Metsamor culture — Wikipedia; Skourtanioti E. et al. (2025). The genetic history of the Southern Caucasus from the Bronze Age to the Early Middle Ages: 5,000 years of genetic continuity despite high mobility. Cell
