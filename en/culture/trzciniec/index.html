Trzciniec culture

The Trzciniec culture of the Bronze Age (19th–11th centuries BCE) covered lands of present-day Poland, Belarus, the Baltic countries and northern and western Ukraine. Józef Kostrzewski identified it in 1930 and named it after a cemetery near Opole Lubelskie. Its people lived by herding and farming; few settlements are known, and they have no fortifications. The dead were usually inhumed, both under barrows and without them. This cultural circle took shape in Poland on the basis of Corded Ware cultures (Mierzanowice, Strzyżów, Iwno) and was influenced by the Srubnaya and other steppe cultures. Mitochondrial genomes from southern Poland showed continuity between the Late Neolithic Corded Ware population and the Trzciniec groups.

Sources: Тшинецкая культура — Википедия; Trzciniec culture — Wikipedia; Juras A. et al. (2020). Mitochondrial genomes from Bronze Age Poland reveal genetic continuity from the Late Neolithic and additional genetic affinities with the steppe populations. American Journal of Physical Anthropology
