Helladic chronology

Helladic chronology is a relative dating system used in archaeology for the Bronze Age of mainland Greece, roughly c. 3200 to c. 1050 BCE. It complements the Minoan scheme devised by Sir Arthur Evans for Crete, and a Cycladic scheme is used for the Aegean islands; the three align chronologically. The systems derive mainly from changes in pottery style. Helladic society has antecedents in Neolithic Greece, whose settlements were small farming villages. The Late Helladic, c. 1550 to c. 1050 BCE, is sometimes called the Mycenaean Age, because Mycenae was then the dominant state in Greece.
