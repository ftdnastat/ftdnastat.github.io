Khvalynsk culture

The Khvalynsk culture belongs to the Eneolithic (5th–4th millennia BCE) and occupies the Middle Volga and the North Caucasus. It is named after the first finds near Khvalynsk in Saratov Oblast. It grew out of the Samara culture but spread more widely: its range reached Saratov in the north, the Sea of Azov in the southwest and the Ural River in the southeast. Its people lived mainly by herding sheep, goats and cattle. Later the culture developed into a local variant of the Yamnaya. The DNA of 435 Eneolithic individuals places the Khvalynsk group among highly heterogeneous populations that arose when people of the Caucasus and the lower Volga mixed with descendants of eastern hunter-gatherers.

Sources: Хвалынская культура — Википедия; Khvalynsk culture — Wikipedia; Lazaridis I. et al. (2025). The genetic origin of the Indo-Europeans. Nature
