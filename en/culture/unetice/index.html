Únětice culture

The Únětice culture was an Early Bronze Age culture of Central Europe, dated roughly to 2300–1600 BCE. Named after a village near Prague, it covered Czechia, Austria, Thuringia, Saxony and north-western Poland. Its economy was based on plough agriculture and animal husbandry; metalsmiths used pure copper as well as its alloys with arsenic, antimony and tin. The dead were buried in flat graves and under barrows, crouched on the side. In Bohemia the Bronze Age saw a population turnover of at least 40%.

Sources: Унетицкая культура — Википедия; Únětice culture — Wikipedia; Papac L. et al. (2021). Dynamic changes in genomic and social structures in third millennium BCE central Europe. Science Advances
