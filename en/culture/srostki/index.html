Srostki culture

The Srostki culture existed in the 9th–10th centuries in the northern Altai and the south of Western Siberia: its sites lie in the western and northern foothills of the Altai and in the adjoining forest-steppe. It takes its name from a cemetery in the village of Srostki near Biysk, Altai Krai. M. P. Gryaznov described it as a separate culture after bringing together, in 1930, the finds of this type known by then. Scholars disagree about who left it: Gryaznov saw it as the outcome of local development, Gavrilova held that it took shape outside the Altai, Arslanova explained it by the mutual influence of local tribes and the Yenisei Kyrgyz, and Savinov linked it to a Kimak-Kipchak population.

Sources: Сросткинская культура — Википедия
