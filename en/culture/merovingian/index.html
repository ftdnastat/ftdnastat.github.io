Merovingian dynasty

The Merovingian dynasty was the ruling family of the Franks from around the middle of the 5th century until Pepin the Short in 751. By 509 they had united all the Franks and the northern Gallo-Romans under their rule, and they went on to conquer most of Gaul, defeating the Visigoths in 507 and the Burgundians in 534. The dynastic name derives from the Salian king Merovech, the father of Childeric I. Long hair set the Merovingians apart from other Franks, and a Merovingian whose hair was cut could not rule. Childeric I was the first well-known king, and his son Clovis I converted to Nicene Christianity. In their last century the kings became largely ceremonial, and real power passed to the mayors of the palace.
