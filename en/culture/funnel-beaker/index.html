Funnel Beaker culture

The Funnel Beaker culture was a Neolithic megalithic culture of north-central Europe, active around 4100–2800 BCE. It stretched from the Elbe catchment and Bohemia, with a western extension into the Netherlands, to southern Scandinavia and the Vistula catchment. It introduced farming and husbandry: barley and emmer were grown and sheep, cattle, pigs and goats kept, while hunting and fishing continued. Beakers and amphorae with funnel-shaped tops were found in dolmen burials, and the megaliths of the region were built mainly in this era. The ancestry of Funnel Beaker farmers on Gotland largely aligns with European Neolithic farmers.

Sources: Культура воронковидных кубков — Википедия; Funnelbeaker culture — Wikipedia; Fraser M. et al. (2026). Ancestry, admixture, and pathogens in contemporaneous Neolithic farmers and foragers on the Island of Gotland. Communications Biology
