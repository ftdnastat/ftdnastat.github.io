El Argar, late period

The El Argar culture is an Early Bronze Age archaeological culture in Europe, named after the El Argar site near Antas in Almería province in southeastern Spain. It is characterized by early use of bronze and sophisticated pottery technology, with the pottery traded around the Mediterranean. The settlement of El Argar, 16,000 m² with about 5,000 inhabitants, was walled; over a thousand graves were found inside the houses. Women were buried with many silver items, suggesting high status. An economic collapse occurred around 1550 BCE. A genetic study showed that with the rise of El Argar around 2200 BCE all Y-chromosome lineages turned over and steppe-related ancestry arrived.

Sources: Эль-Аргарская культура — Википедия; El Argar — Wikipedia; Villalba-Mouco V. et al. (2021). Genomic transformation and social organization during the Copper Age–Bronze Age transition in southern Iberia. Science Advances
