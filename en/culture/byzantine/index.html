Byzantine Empire

Byzantium, or the Eastern Roman Empire, continued the Roman Empire in its eastern provinces in late antiquity and the Middle Ages; its capital was Constantinople. It survived the fall of the Western Roman Empire in the 5th century and lasted until 1453, when Constantinople was taken by the Ottomans. Its inhabitants called themselves Romans, and the names Byzantine Empire and Eastern Roman Empire appeared only after the state had perished. Constantine I moved the capital to Constantinople and legalized Christianity; under Justinian I (527–565) the empire was at its largest. In 1204 Constantinople was sacked by the participants of the Fourth Crusade. Ancient DNA has shown a rare continuity of the population of Anatolia down to the Roman and Byzantine periods.

Sources: Византия — Википедия; Byzantine Empire — Wikipedia; Lazaridis I. et al. (2022). A genetic probe into the ancient and medieval history of Southern Europe and West Asia. Science
