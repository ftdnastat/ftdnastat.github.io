Srubnaya culture

The Srubnaya cultural-historical community unites archaeological cultures of the Late Bronze Age (18th–13th centuries BCE) in the steppe and forest-steppe of Eastern Europe from the Dnieper to the Urals. It was identified by V. A. Gorodtsov in 1901–1903 and takes its name from the srub, a timber frame inside the grave. The dead were laid in pits or timber frames, flexed on the left side; cremations occur as well. Paleogeneticists studied a kurgan of the Srubnaya-Alakul tradition in the southern Urals, about 3,800 years old: descent there was reckoned through the father, and wives came from a wider circle of communities.

Sources: Срубная культурно-историческая общность — Википедия; Srubnaya culture — Wikipedia; Blöcher J. et al. (2023). Descent, marriage, and residence practices of a 3,800-year-old pastoral community in Central Eurasia. PNAS
