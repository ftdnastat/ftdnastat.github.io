Roman people, late period

Romans were the citizens of Rome, in the time of the kings, of the republic and of the empire alike. At first only the Latins of the city itself were citizens; by the 1st century BCE citizenship had reached the other peoples of Italy, and in 212 an edict of Emperor Caracalla extended it to all free inhabitants of the empire. At the height of their power the Romans ruled vast lands in Europe, the Near East and North Africa. A study of 127 genomes from Rome and its surroundings found that in the imperial period the city received a flow of immigrants from the Near East, followed later by a growing genetic contribution from Europe.

Sources: Римляне — Википедия; Roman people — Wikipedia; Antonio M. L. et al. (2019). Ancient Rome: A genetic crossroads of Europe and the Mediterranean. Science
