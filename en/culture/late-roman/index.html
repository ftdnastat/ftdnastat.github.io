Roman people, late period

The Romans were the body of Roman citizens during the Roman Kingdom, the Roman Republic, and the Roman Empire. Roman citizenship originally belonged only to ethnic Latins from Rome itself and was extended to the rest of the Italic peoples by the 1st century BCE. Emperor Caracalla's edict of 212 CE extended citizenship rights to all free inhabitants of the empire. At their peak, the Romans ruled large parts of Europe, the Near East, and North Africa. A study of 127 genomes from Rome and its surroundings showed that during the Imperial period the city's population received net immigration from the Near East, followed by an increase in genetic contributions from Europe.

Sources: Римляне — Википедия; Roman people — Wikipedia; Antonio M. L. et al. (2019). Ancient Rome: A genetic crossroads of Europe and the Mediterranean. Science
