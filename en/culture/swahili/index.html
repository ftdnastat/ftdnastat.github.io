Swahili people

The Swahili are a Bantu-group people living mainly on the coast of East Africa (in Tanzania, including Zanzibar, Kenya, northern Mozambique and southern Somalia). They descend from the indigenous population of the coast and islands, who mixed with Indians, Arabs and Persians settling there. In the Middle Ages they formed the ethnic basis of the city-states of Kilwa, Pate, Malindi and others. Ancient DNA of 80 people from six medieval and early modern coastal towns (1250–1800 CE) showed that many derive over half their DNA from mainly female African ancestors and a large share from Asian ancestors, with 80–90% of the Asian DNA from Persian men.

Sources: Суахили (народ) — Википедия; Swahili people — Wikipedia; Brielle E. S. et al. (2023). Entwined African and Asian genetic roots of medieval peoples of the Swahili coast. Nature
