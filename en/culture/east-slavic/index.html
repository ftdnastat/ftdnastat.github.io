East Slavs

The East Slavs are a subgroup of the Slavs who speak East Slavic languages and live chiefly in Eastern Europe, though they also extend across northern Asia and to the Pacific. The Russian-language article places the start of East Slavic history at the time when a separate East Slavic language began to split from Proto-Slavic, in the 7th–8th centuries on linguistic evidence. They formed the majority of the population of the medieval state of Kievan Rus', which they regard as their cultural ancestor. The existing East Slavic groups are Belarusians, Russians and Ukrainians; by the 17th century these three peoples had taken shape, in descending order of size. Rusyns and Don Cossacks are sometimes considered a separate nation, though they are often seen as a subgroup of Ukrainians and Russians.
