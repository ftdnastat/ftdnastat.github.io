Sagly-Bazhy culture

The Sagly-Bazhy culture, also called Sagly/Uyuk and known in Mongolia as the Chandman culture, is the Saka culture of the Sayan Mountains in modern Tuva. It is the last stage of the Uyuk culture and covers the 5th to 2nd centuries BCE, following the Arzhan culture (8th century BCE) and the Aldy-Bel culture (7th–6th centuries BCE) in the same place. Nearby Saka cultures included the Tagar culture of the Minusinsk Basin and the Pazyryk culture of the Altai Mountains (c. 500–200 BCE), and the Slab-grave culture lay to the east. The Sagly-Bazhy culture ceased to exist in the 2nd century BCE as a result of Xiongnu invasions, and these Scythian cultures were ultimately replaced by the Xiongnu Empire and the Kokel culture.
