Qijia culture

The Qijia culture (2400 BCE – 1600 BCE) was an early Bronze Age culture distributed around the upper Yellow River region of Gansu, centred on Lanzhou, and eastern Qinghai, China. It is regarded as one of the earliest bronze cultures in China and is named after the Qijiaping Site in Gansu Province. Before it, the same area was home to the Majiayao culture, which was also familiar with metalwork. At the end of the third millennium BCE the Qijia culture succeeded Majiayao at sites in three main zones: eastern Gansu, middle Gansu and western Gansu with eastern Qinghai. It benefited from warm and humid conditions that fostered agriculture and rapid population growth, and declined with the later aridification.
