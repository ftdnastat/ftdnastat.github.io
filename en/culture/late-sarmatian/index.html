Sarmatians, late period

The Sarmatians were a large confederation of ancient Iranian equestrian nomadic peoples who inhabited the steppe belt of Eurasia from the Danube to the Aral Sea; they are usually dated from the 5th–4th century BCE to the 4th century CE. They practiced nomadic pastoralism. Burials were made in kurgans, in rectangular pits, with the body laid extended on the back, head to the south. The Sarmatians were renowned for their heavy cavalry, the cataphracts. They dominated the Pontic Steppe from the 3rd century BCE and the Great Hungarian Plain from 50 CE until the Huns' expansion in the 4th century. A study of 156 genomes showed that Sarmatians of the Carpathian Basin descended from Steppe Sarmatians from the Ural and Kazakhstan regions.

Sources: Сарматы — Википедия; Sarmatians — Wikipedia; Schütz O. et al. (2025). Unveiling the origins and genetic makeup of the "forgotten people": A study of the Sarmatian-period population in the Carpathian Basin. Cell
