Maykop culture

The Maykop culture is a major Bronze Age archaeological culture of the Caucasus foothills, dated in the English-language article to about 3700–3000 BCE. It is named after the great Maykop kurgan, excavated in 1897 by N. I. Veselovsky. Its core lay on the plains and foothills of the Ciscaucasus from the Taman Peninsula to Chechnya; to the south it climbs into the foothills along river valleys and reaches the Black Sea coast only between Taman and Gelendzhik. Scholars also single out a Kuma-Manych periphery and a broad area where Maykop-style goods are found. According to ancient DNA studies published in 2018, the population came from the south, from Imereti, and descended from Chalcolithic farmers who first settled the northern side of the Caucasus.

Sources: Майкопская культура — Википедия; Maykop culture — Wikipedia
