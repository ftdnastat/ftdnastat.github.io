Rus' people

The Rus' were a people of early medieval Eastern Europe. The scholarly consensus is that they were originally Norsemen, mainly from present-day Sweden, who settled and ruled along the river routes between the Baltic and the Black Sea from about the 8th to the 11th centuries. Their two original centres were Ladoga, founded in the mid-8th century, and Rurikovo Gorodische, founded in the mid-9th century, at opposite ends of the Volkhov River. From there the name passed to the Middle Dnieper, and the newly formed state became known as Kievan Rus'. The ruling Norsemen and local Finnic tribes gradually assimilated into the East Slavic population; Old Norse remained familiar to the elite until the second half of the 11th century. The Rus' gave their name to Russia and Belarus.

Sources: Русь (народ) — Википедия; Rus' people — Wikipedia
