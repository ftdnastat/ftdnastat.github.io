Kura–Araxes culture

The Kura–Araxes culture, also called the Early Transcaucasian culture, existed from about 4000 BCE until about 2000 BCE, though in some places it may have disappeared as early as 2600 or 2700 BCE. Its earliest evidence comes from the Ararat plain in the Armenian highlands, and it spread north into the Caucasus by 3000 BCE. It covered an area of about 1,000 by 500 km, including Armenia, eastern Georgia, Azerbaijan, northwestern Iran, the northeastern Caucasus and eastern Turkey. The culture had a sedentary agricultural economy with more than a thousand settlements, and its Early Bronze Age hill-settlements show multiple cultural layers. It gave rise to the Trialeti culture of the South Caucasus.

Sources: Куро-араксская культура — Википедия; Kura–Araxes culture — Wikipedia
