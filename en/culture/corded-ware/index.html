Corded Ware culture

The Corded Ware culture was a broad archaeological horizon of Europe in the Late Neolithic to Early Bronze Age, usually dated to about 2900–2300 BCE. It encompassed most of continental northern Europe from the Rhine in the west to the Volga in the east. Its bearers practiced herding of cattle, sheep and pigs, grew wheat and barley, and used wheeled vehicles. The dead were buried under tumuli, with a stone battle axe typical in men's graves; pottery was decorated with cord impressions. Ancient DNA shows that Corded Ware people from Germany traced about 75% of their ancestry to the Yamnaya culture.
