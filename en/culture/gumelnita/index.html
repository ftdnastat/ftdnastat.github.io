Gumelnița culture

The Gumelnița culture was an Eneolithic culture of settled farmers of the Balkan Peninsula that existed in the 5th millennium BCE. It is named after the Gumelnița tell on the left bank of the Danube in Romania. Its sites are known in northern and southeastern Bulgaria and in Muntenia and Dobruja in Romania; in Bulgaria it is called Kodžadermen-Karanovo VI. The culture is distinguished by massive copper tools and weapons and by a wealth of human and animal figures. According to paleogenetics, the Gumelnița–Kodžadermen–Karanovo VI complex was held together by a broad cultural unity rather than by close genetic kinship.

Sources: Гумельницкая культура — Википедия; Gumelnița culture — Wikipedia; Penske S. et al. (2023). Early contact between late farming and pastoralist societies in southeastern Europe. Nature
