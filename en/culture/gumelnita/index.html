Gumelnița culture

The Gumelnița culture is an archaeological culture of the Eneolithic (5th millennium BCE) of settled farming tribes of the Balkan Peninsula, named after the Gumelnița tell in Romania on the left bank of the Danube. It is found in northern and southeastern Bulgaria and in Muntenia and Dobruja in Romania; in Bulgaria it is called Kodžadermen-Karanovo VI. The culture is characterized by massive copper tools and weapons. It is remarkable for the richness of its anthropomorphic and zoomorphic representations. A paleogenetic study found that the Gumelnița–Kodžadermen–Karanovo VI complex reflects wide cultural rather than close genetic connectedness.

Sources: Гумельницкая культура — Википедия; Gumelnița culture — Wikipedia; Penske S. et al. (2023). Early contact between late farming and pastoralist societies in southeastern Europe. Nature
