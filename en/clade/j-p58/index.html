Equivalent branches, the same kits: J-Z1874 (13,955 kits, 12k years)

J-P58 is a branch of the FamilyTreeDNA Y tree with 13,955 kits that gave a country, counting all sub-branches. Almost all of its kits sit in a single sub-branch, J-CTS9721, so this branch gets no conclusions of its own. Stands out most: Saudi Arabia (47%), Yemen (49%), Jordan (43%). The branch's common ancestor lived about 9,800 years ago: the FTDNA Discover estimate runs from 8,600 to 12k years ago. The oldest of the equivalent branches, J-Z1874, split off about 12k years ago by Discover. YFull has 9,091 samples in this branch and dates its common ancestor to 9,200 years ago.

Haber et al. (2017) write that P58 is frequent in the Arabian Peninsula and has proposed origins in the Zagros and Taurus mountains. According to the paper, it makes up the vast majority of Y chromosomes in southwestern Mesopotamia and reaches 74.1% among the Marsh Arabs of Iraq. One of the two Bronze Age men from Sidon also carried P58. Wikipedia adds that it is not common among the J1 of the Caucasus.
