R-Z93 is a branch of the FamilyTreeDNA Y tree with 3,164 kits that gave a country, counting all sub-branches. It is most common in these samples: Russia (Republic of Karachay-Cherkessia) (33% of kits), Russia (Republic of Adygea) (27% of kits), Pakistan (25% of kits). The branch's common ancestor lived about 4,700 years ago: the FTDNA Discover estimate runs from 4,100 to 5,400 years ago. YFull has 1,760 samples in this branch and dates its common ancestor to 4,700 years ago.

Underhill's team splits R1a into a European and an Asian part, with Z93 the main Asian branch. The common ancestor of both parts lived about 5,800 years ago. Haak and colleagues showed that Corded Ware people in Germany drew three quarters of their ancestry from the Yamnaya. A mass migration came from the steppe, and with it, the authors argue, at least some Indo-European languages. The Fatyanovo people were Corded Ware folk of what is now western Russia. Saag's team found in them not the European Z283 but Z93, in all six men whose branch could be determined. Allentoft's group thinks the Sintashta culture of the southern Urals most likely arose when Corded Ware people moved east; the Andronovo people, they add, are genetically close to Sintashta. In the data of Narasimhan and colleagues, every Middle and Late Bronze Age man from the Central Steppe, today's Kazakhstan, carried Z93. Between 2000 and 1000 BC people of the same ancestry moved into South Asia. This, the authors say, is why Balto-Slavic and Indo-Iranian languages share features.
