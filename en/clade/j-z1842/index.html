J-Z1842 is a branch of the FamilyTreeDNA Y tree with 1,384 kits that gave a country, counting all sub-branches. Stands out most: Dagestan, Russia (53%); Chechnya, Russia (19%); Orstkhoy (Aosta Valley in FTDNA) (15%). The branch's common ancestor lived about 6,400 years ago: the FTDNA Discover estimate runs from 5,600 to 7,400 years ago. The oldest of the equivalent branches, J-Z18426, split off about 7,900 years ago by Discover. YFull has 479 samples in this branch and dates its common ancestor to 7,700 years ago.

Agdzhoyan et al. (2023) describe the most common branch of the East Caucasus, J1-Y3495. In the FTDNA tree its SNP is listed as Z18429 and sits on the Z1842 node. The paper dates the branch to 6.4 ± 0.6 thousand years, traces most of the J1 male lines of Dagestan back to it and suggests it may have arisen in central Dagestan. A man from East Anatolia who lived about 5,000 years ago belonged, according to the paper, to Z1842.
